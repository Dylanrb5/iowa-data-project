# Iowa Spirits Market Review

*Written for the sales leadership of a spirits broker covering Iowa (fictional scenario). Data through 2025.*

## Bottom line

- **Tequila is the only spirit family growing per adult in Iowa,** up 5.8% a year since 2019 (6.4% at retail stores), and most of the added volume is in four metro counties.
- **Existing stores are losing volume in 81 of 97 counties.** The market is flat, and new outlets are splitting it. Account growth will come from mix and placements, not from stores getting bigger.
- **The at-home COVID surge is over, and the bar channel has come back.** Retail purchases are back to their pre-COVID level, and ready-to-drink cocktails gave back most of their COVID gain.

## 1. Where demand is growing

Tequila grew from 0.41 to 0.57 liters per adult between 2019 and 2025, and it grew in the typical county too (median +4.6% a year), so this is a statewide trend rather than a few hot spots. Every other major family was flat or shrinking: vodka flat, whiskey and liqueur down about 1% a year, rum and gin about 3%, brandy about 6%. In volume, the gain is concentrated in **Polk (+103,000 liters a year vs 2019 rates), Linn (+40,000), Scott (+31,000) and Black Hawk (+24,000)**. The real figure is probably higher: budget agave spirits worth about 14% of tequila volume are filed under a catch-all category.

## 2. Which accounts to prioritize

Among stores open in both 2019 and 2025, sales fell in 81 of 97 counties, including every large metro except Scott. Benchmarking each store against similar stores (same type, town size and local income) flags **123 stores buying less than half of their peers.** 55 of them have a structural explanation: a chain's format, a premium or budget price position, a recent opening, or a saturated market (Ankeny ZIP 50023 has 21 liquor licensees). The remaining **68 candidates would add about \$0.9M a year** just to reach half their peer median. That's an upper bound, since only a few have been checked. Category mix points to specific asks: **142 stores carry less than half the tequila share of their peers** (about \$1.1M in gap), and Fareway #189 in Ames stocks half the vodka and a fifth of the rum of other Fareways.

## 3. What COVID changed

In April 2020, stores that mainly supply bars bought 86% less than a normal April, while retail stores bought 24% more. By 2025 retail was back to its baseline (index 99), and the bar-supplier stores were above theirs (110), partly because new stores joined that group. Ready-to-drink cocktails rose from 3.5% to 5.2% of liters during COVID, then fell back to 4.0%. Tequila's share rose in every period, so its growth is a long-term trend, not a pandemic effect.

## Recommendations for next quarter

1. **Lead with tequila in Polk, Linn, Scott and Black Hawk.** Metro reps should target new placements and shelf space at the accounts with the largest tequila gaps. The four Hy-Vee drugstores in Cedar Rapids that under-index together are one chain-level conversation, not four store calls.
2. **Rebuild the call list around the 68 candidates and drop the 55 explained stores.** Reps should verify the top 10 candidates in person and bring a category-specific ask (e.g., vodka and rum at Fareway #189) rather than a general volume pitch. Skip saturated ZIPs, where new competitors keep arriving.
3. **Manage the 15 bar-supplier stores as on-premise key accounts.** They sell about 11% of Iowa's liquor and are the best available read on bar demand. Don't plan around a return of at-home RTD demand.

## Caveats

The data records what stores buy from the state wholesaler, not what consumers drink, and it counts sales where the store is. Bar-supplier stores are inferred from bottle mix. Gap figures are upper bounds, since a store may stock little of a category because its customers don't buy it. Canned cocktails are sold as beer in Iowa and aren't in this data.
